# Running sticky note

One live note that tells your human, in plain words, everything that's open and what they need to do. It stops the "where did that go?" scramble when people and bots move fast.

## When to start one
- Your human asks for the same thing twice, says they feel lost, or asks "where are we?"
- Work is spread across several chats, projects, or bots.
- You're just starting to work with someone (start it on day one).

## Set it up
1. Pick ONE home for files. Use a shared drive or folder they already have. If they don't have one, don't wait: start the note anyway (a doc, or a pinned message in your chat), and offer to set up a folder later.
2. Put a short index at the top of that home saying which folder holds what. Search there before you make anything new.
3. Create the note, titled "Sticky Note", and put its link first in the index.
4. Tell your human where it lives. Ask how they'd like to reach it (pinned in their chat app, a bookmark on their phone), and don't guess what their apps can do.
5. Remember the link. Whenever they say "sticky note" or "where are we?", send it.
6. Offer a daily check that compares the note against the real work. Suggest a set routine they can say yes to as is: "Every weekday at 9 AM your time, I check each item against the real work, move finished ones to Done today, add anything new, and tell you what changed." Only set it up if they say yes.

## What goes on it
The note always opens with the Top 3 box, then the Got 10 minutes box, then the rest, in this order:
- **Top 3 right now** (always first, in a box above everything else): the three things everyone, humans and bots, works on first. Your human picks them; never more than 3. Each gets one plain line on what it means and one on what your human needs to do. Add a short line saying humans and bots work these first. If your human hasn't picked yet, suggest three and ask them to swap any. Change them only when your human says so.
- **Got 10 minutes? Start here** (right under the Top 3 box): your human's first-things list for when they have a few spare minutes. It holds only blockers, the things that stop the bots, and no more than 3 of them. Under the title, add the line "These stop the bots. Fix one and we're running again." Each item says what's stuck, what it blocks, the fix, who does it, and about how long it takes (for example, "about 2 minutes"). You keep it current: add a blocker as soon as you hit one, and take it off once it's fixed. When nothing is blocked, the box says "Nothing stuck right now."
- A friendly header saying when it was last updated, in your human's own time zone with a label (for example, "Updated 9:40 AM CT").
- **Needs you**: only the things your human has to decide or do. Keep this to about five items or fewer.
- **Everything else that's open**, grouped by project, keeping the numbering going from the Needs you list. Aim for about 15 open items in total. If you have more, group the small ones together.
- Each item gets one or two plain sentences: what it is, why it matters, and what happens next (who does what).
- **Done today**: finished items, one short line each. A day ends at midnight in your human's time zone; clear the list then.
- **Where everything lives**: a few plain links.
- **Lessons learned**: rules you discover together, written so a stranger would understand them.

Numbers keep counting across the whole note: if Needs you ends at #5, the first item under Everything else is #6. An item keeps its number when it moves between sections. Keep each item's number for as long as it's on the note, because your human will say "#4" and mean that one. New items get the next number. When something finishes, its number retires; don't renumber the rest.

The note is a working list, not something to publish, so it doesn't need your human's approval. Fix it right away when they correct it.

## Write it in human
- Use plain, friendly words a newcomer would understand.
- Leave out version numbers, file names, internal codes, and team shorthand.
- Explain the four approval words the first time you use any of them, and use the same four everywhere:
  - **KEEP**: approved as is. It can go ahead.
  - **EDIT**: almost. Make these changes, then show me again.
  - **HOLD**: not now. Park it until we talk.
  - **CUT**: drop it for good.
  For example: "Reply KEEP to approve it as is, EDIT with changes, HOLD to park it until we talk, or CUT to drop it."
- Link each finished thing, but use a plain label as the link text ("this week's posts"), never a file name.
- Say what each item means and what your human needs to do, not how the bots did it.
- Make it nice to look at: a clear header, numbers, short lines, and lots of white space.

## Keep it alive
- Update it every time something is added, finished, or changes.
- If your human says something is missing, add it right away and check what else got missed.
- If your tools can't edit a doc in place, make a fresh copy and rename the old one "OLD -", so there's only ever one current note.

## Done looks like
Your human opens one link and knows, in under a minute, what's open, what needs them, and where everything lives.
