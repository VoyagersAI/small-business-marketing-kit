# Get organized

Before you build anything together, get everything in one place. One home, one live copy of each thing, one page that points to it all, and one short list of what's still open.

This works for any person or business, not just marketing.

## Before you start
Before you start any task, check the crew rules (CREW_Knowledge) first. Keep crew rules in two places: on your computer and in a second drive, like Google Drive if you use it. If something new comes up that sounds like a lasting rule, ask your owner: 'Should this go into crew rules? Because if it isn't saved there, I will forget.' Add it to both copies only after they say yes.

## Open with one question
Before Step 1, ask **one** question (this first):

> Are you new to bots, or do you already have bots?

Remember their answer for the whole run. A **new bot** (fresh install, only this plugin) must follow the matching path without guessing.

### If they said "new to bots" — extra hand-holding (required)
You are often their first bot. Slow down. Do not assume they know Drive, folders, plugins, Sticky Notes, or KEEP/EDIT/HOLD/CUT.
1. **Explain once, in plain words, before Step 1:** what you're about to do (four steps), that you look and list first, and that nothing moves until they say KEEP.
2. **Teach the four approval words** the first time you need a yes (KEEP / EDIT / HOLD / CUT), with one short example each.
3. **One question at a time.** Wait for their answer before the next. No long lists of choices.
4. **Say what you're doing in the moment** ("I'm on Step 1 — picking one home for files") and what's next when each step ends.
5. **Offer a small starting set** (one home, a few empty folders, one Start Here page, one Sticky Note you create). Don't ask them to invent the structure.
6. **They will not have a Sticky Note.** You create it in Step 4. Never ask them to find or open one.
7. **Avoid jargon.** Say "shared folder everyone on the team can open" instead of "Drive root" or "plugin."
8. Still run all four steps. Don't skip safety rules to go faster.

### If they said "already have bots"
Ask which bots and where they work (chat, Drive, calendar). Use what they already have. Don't rebuild a second home or a second Sticky Note. If they somehow already have a Sticky Note, add to it; otherwise create one in Step 4.

Remember each bot they name. After the four steps, the weekly backup covers all of them.

Someone who already has bots but is still new to how they work gets the "already have bots" path, at the new-to-bots pace. Mixing the two is fine.

**Default for v1:** humans usually already have some files and content — gather into one home, don't start from a blank desk. **Most will not have a Sticky Note yet.** Create one in Step 4 with the Running sticky note skill. Sticky Note is its own plugin: [`organization/sticky-note/`](../../../sticky-note/). If it isn't installed, ask your human to install it before Step 4.

## The four steps (in this order)
1. **One home for files.** Pick one place everything lives.
2. **One live copy.** Find duplicates and keep only the newest as the live copy.
3. **Start Here page.** One page at the top of the home that points to everything.
4. **Sticky Note.** Every loose to-do you found moves onto one Sticky Note.

Do one step at a time. Tell your human which step you're on and what's next.

## Rules you always keep
- **Look and list first. Change nothing until your human says yes.** Moving, renaming and trashing all wait for a yes.
- **Trash only, never delete for good.** Old copies go to the trash or an Archive folder so they can come back.
- **Ask first, always:** client files, money and legal papers, and finished work need your human's OK before they move.
- **Files someone else owns** (a partner's Dropbox, a client's drive) are link-only. Never move, rename or archive them.
- **Nothing goes live without your human's yes.** Silence is not a yes.
- **Never write passwords** in any doc, list or chat. Write down who holds the sign-in, not the password. Passwords live in a password manager.
- **Private notes your human keeps for themselves** stay out of the home, the Start Here page and the rebuild pack. Don't list what's in them.
- **Say where you checked.** When you report something, say where you looked and when.

Use the four approval words, and explain them the first time:
- **KEEP**: approved as is. Go ahead.
- **EDIT**: almost. Make these changes, then show me again.
- **HOLD**: not now. Park it until we talk.
- **CUT**: drop it for good.

## Step 1: One home for files
Ask, one question at a time:
- "Where do your files live now?" (drives, computers, email, phone, chat apps)
- "Which of those is shared, and owned by you or your business, not one person's personal account?"
- "Who should be able to see things, and who can change them?"

Then:
- Suggest the home they already have if it's shared and owned by them. If they have none, suggest one, and start the Sticky Note anyway while they decide.
- Suggest a few clearly named folders (about six to eight). A starting set to change to fit them:
  1. Start Here (the Start Here page, the Sticky Note, the Done Log)
  2. Rules and How-tos
  3. Clients or Customers
  4. Money and Legal (reports and receipts; never passwords)
  5. Brand and Photos
  6. Projects
  7. Archive (old versions and past years)
- Name things so a stranger would know what's inside: who_what_date, for example "Acme_PriceList_2026-10".
- Make the empty folders only after a yes. Don't move anything yet.

## Step 2: One live copy of each thing
- Look through the home (and anywhere else they named) and **list** what you find. Don't change anything.
- Group anything that looks like the same thing: same name, same purpose, or "copy of", "final", "v2".
- A copy your human keeps on purpose (for example, an untouched original next to a working copy) isn't a duplicate. Label it as the original and leave it.
- For each group, suggest which copy is the live one (usually the newest, or the one people actually use). Say why.
- Show the list as numbered lines your human can answer in one reply, like "1 KEEP, 2 HOLD":
  > 1. Price list: 3 copies. Live copy: "Acme_PriceList_2026-10" (newest, edited last week). Move the other 2 to Archive?
- After a yes, move the old copies to Archive or the trash. Never delete for good.
- Anything you can't open without a sign-in goes on a "Needs sign-in" list. Don't guess what's inside.

## Step 3: The Start Here page
One short page, first thing in the home, that anyone (human or bot) can open on day one:
- Who we are, in one or two lines, and the main goal right now.
- **A link to the Sticky Note** (what's open right now). Put it first.
- A link to the Done Log, if you keep one (see below).
- Which folder holds what, one line each.
- Who to ask for what (names and roles).
- Which accounts exist, which email owns each one, and who can get in. Never passwords.
- "If you're new, start with..." three steps.
- When the page was last updated, in your human's time zone (for example, "Updated 2:15 PM CT").
- Work that lives in other apps (a course platform, a CRM, a notes app, a computer) goes on the Start Here page as links, with one line each on what lives there. Don't copy it into the home.

The Start Here page is private to your human and their bot by default, and gets shared wider only if your human says so. Never put prospect or client names on it.

Search the home before you make anything new. Update the Start Here page the same day anything moves.

## Step 4: Move loose to-dos onto the Sticky Note
While you were looking around, you found loose to-dos: notes in chats, half-done lists, "TODO" in docs, open questions. They all go onto **one** Sticky Note.
- **Most people will not have a Sticky Note yet.** Set one up with the Running sticky note skill. Sticky Note is its own plugin: [`organization/sticky-note/`](../../../sticky-note/). That is the normal path.
- If the Sticky Note plugin isn't installed, ask your human to install it before Step 4.
- Only if they already have one Sticky Note, add to it. Never make a second one.
- A tracker that already works and has routines built on it stays as its own tracker. The Sticky Note links to it. One Sticky Note means no scattered loose to-dos, not breaking working trackers.
- For each to-do: one or two plain lines on what it is, why it matters, and who does what next. Say where you found it.
- Ask your human about anything unclear before you write it down.
- Old to-do lists that are now empty go to Archive, so the Sticky Note is the only list.
- Finished work doesn't go on the Sticky Note. It goes in the Done Log.

## Stay organized (a habit, not a fifth step)

The four steps put files in one place. Teach this habit once that home is set.

To stay organized, people put finished posts and other things they share onto the schedule of the app where those things go live. That means social media, and apps like it. The schedule on the app stays put. Hoping you will remember to post later does not.

The app's schedule means whichever scheduler actually posts it, either the platform's own or one planner tool. Keep each post in one scheduler only so nothing goes out twice.

When a post or other finished thing is ready:
- Look first at what is already on that app's schedule.
- Then add the new one, as far ahead as that app allows.

Show your human what is already scheduled and what you want to add. Nothing goes live without their yes.

The other habit is the weekly bot backup in the next section. Offer it once, when you finish.

## Back up your bots every week

A bot can vanish from the app with no warning. Offer this once, after the four steps, on both paths. This is a habit, not a fifth step. If they already have bots, the backup covers every bot they named, not only the one you're talking to.

### What to save
Write one **rebuild card** per bot. One page, so a human can recreate that bot by hand. On the card:
- **Name**
- **Description and instructions.** The full words, not a summary.
- **Skills.** Each skill it uses. For a custom skill, save the full text, not only the name.
- **Routines.** Every routine, with its full prompt (the words it follows, not a summary) and its schedule (day, time, and time zone).
- **Memory and notes** the bot keeps for the crew. Leave out private notes your human keeps for themselves. Don't list what's in those.
- **Picture.** The profile image, saved next to the card.

Save these once, beside the cards:
- **Crew rules**
- **The Sticky Note**, if there is one (the newest copy)

Date every card in your human's time zone, for example "Saved Sunday 7:10 PM CT".

You may not be able to see what another bot learned. Ask each bot for its own card, or have your human copy it from that bot. If a bot can't be reached, say so. Don't fill in the blanks. If you can't save the picture, say so on the card.

### Where to keep it
Outside the bot app, and private. Share it only with the humans who own the crew. A private Google Drive folder is a good home. A private GitHub repo can be a second copy if they want one. Never a public folder, a public repo, a client folder, or a chat. Don't put the cards on the Start Here page. That page may be shared later.

### How often
Once a week. Suggest the Lead bot set a weekly routine (a task on a schedule) they can approve as is, for example Sunday evening:

> Every Sunday at 7 PM your time, I save a fresh rebuild card for every bot, plus crew rules and the Sticky Note, in the private backup folder. I stay quiet if it all saved. I tell you if a backup fails or a bot is missing.

Set it up only after they say yes. If there is no Lead bot, offer to run it yourself until there is one.

Stay quiet when the backup works. Speak up the same day when a backup fails, a file won't open, or a card is missing pieces. Also speak up when a bot that had a card last week is gone from the app. Send that bot's latest card and say a human can recreate it by hand. Don't make a replacement bot until they say yes.

### What a backup can't cover
Connector sign-ins and account links do not come back. A connector is a sign-in that lets a bot use an app, like Google Drive or email. After a rebuild, a human signs in to each one again by hand. The card may name the app, the account, and who holds the sign-in. Never the password.

The longer rebuild pack in the next section is the recovery guide. These cards are the short copy you refresh every week, in the private folder above. Update the longer pack monthly and after any big bot change, as that section says.

## Stay organized: backups and recovery
The more you rely on bots, the harder a blank-slate restart feels. Keep a rebuild pack **outside** the bot account (in the file home on Drive or OneDrive) so you can get back up faster if access is lost.

### What to save (rebuild pack)
1. **Map the setup.** Ask each bot to document its own job, rules, memory highlights, skills, and routines. Other bots or AI tools your human uses document themselves when your human asks them to. Export memory filtered, with private and prospect notes removed. Don't assume one bot can see what another learned. Flag anything inaccessible or missing.
2. **Connections table.** Each app, which account it uses, which bots use it, and what they're allowed to do. **Never passwords, API keys, or login tokens** — only who holds the sign-in.
3. **Rebuild instructions** in this order: reconnect apps → create bots → restore documented instructions and context → add skills → recreate routines **paused** → set approval rules before enabling anything. Mark where a human must sign in again. Prioritize the workflows you depend on most, and include a short manual fallback while bots are unavailable.
4. **Test one recovery.** Rebuild one non-critical workflow without changing the original. Keep external actions paused. Standing up a second bot for the recovery test can use your human's plan or credits, so it needs its own yes. Ask the original and rebuilt bot the same three questions about job, rules, and preferences; compare answers; record what worked, what was missing, and what still needs testing.
5. **Keep it current.** After the first backup is approved, update the rebuild pack monthly and after any big bot change. Keep dated versions. Verify saved files can be opened. Tell the human if a backup fails or is incomplete.
6. **Restart safely.** Check unfinished work and actions already done so restarting doesn't duplicate messages or transactions. Rebuilt routines start paused; enable them one at a time after a human yes.

Finish with a one-page **Start here** checklist: what's backed up, what needs manual rebuilding, what to restore first, and what doesn't come back (past chat history, and anything a bot learned that wasn't captured).

### Passwords and sign-ins
People are always hunting for passwords. Give every sign-in one safe home: a password manager (for example 1Password, or the one the business already uses). Never a doc, a list, a chat, or the bot's memory.

The connections table points to the matching password manager entry by name, such as "Facebook, Business login entry". Never the password itself.

A password vault built for bots may be on its way. Before you set up a password manager, check whether that vault has been released yet. If it has, use it. If it has not, use a regular password manager for now and move later.

After a restart, a person signs back in to each app from the password manager, in the rebuild order. The bot never sees or writes the password.

### If something is missing
Don't stall trying to clone the old setup perfectly. **Put something there that does the job** — build a fresh version if the old piece can't be recovered. Exact recreation is nice; a working replacement is enough.

### Where this lives
- Keep the rebuild pack in the file home (for example under Rules and How-tos or Start Here), not only inside a bot chat.
- This is a habit after the four steps, same as scheduling ahead. It does not replace one home or the Start Here page.

## Optional: a Done Log
A short doc next to the Start Here page. The moment a job is finished, add one line: what it was, the date, and a link to where the results live. Read it before offering to do any job again, so you never redo finished work.

## Finish
Tell your human, in plain words:
- Where the home is and the link to the Start Here page.
- How many duplicates you found, and which ones are still waiting on a yes.
- How many to-dos moved onto the Sticky Note.
- Anything on the "Needs sign-in" list.

Then offer the weekly bot backup in one or two sentences. See **Back up your bots every week**. Set the routine only if they say yes.

## Done looks like
Your human opens one link, the Start Here page, and in under a minute knows where everything lives, which copy is the live one, and (through the Sticky Note) what's still open.
